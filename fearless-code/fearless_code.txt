When you think, your brain cells (neurons) communicate with each other via electromagnetic impulses. These impulses travel across synapses – the connections between brain cells – creating what are called neuronal connections: lines of electromagnetic communication. Just to give you a sense of how complex this is: your brain consists of approximately 50 to 100 billion neurons, connected by up to 100 trillion synapses. When you think about a topic for the first time, or only once, there are few – if any – existing neuronal connections related to that topic. Very few brain cells are talking to each other about it. However, the more frequently you think the same thoughts, the more connections form. And: the more frequently you revisit the same thoughts (think the same over and over again), the denser and more branched these connections become. If you keep on intensively thinking these identical thoughts over and over again, you end up with many strong neuronal connections. The processing of any new information related to that topic will become faster – and eventually automatic. Many brain cells are now actively communicating about this topic. This also explains how you actually learn a language: at first, you have to consciously think about every single word. What’s the translation of word you are looking for in the new language? The more you practice (frequently think the same thoughts - same translation of that word), the less thinking - to find that particular word - is required. And if you go on to repeatedly think those identical thoughts (in context to the word you’re translating), then, at some point, you’ll speak almost effortlessly – because you’ve developed strong, well-connected neural networks. A language is essentially just a collection of strong, complex neural networks – in other words: beliefs.
